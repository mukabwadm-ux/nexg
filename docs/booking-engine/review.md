# Decisions, consistency and acceptance review

Date: 29 October 2026. Status: proposed design prepared for review. Application acceptance and runtime behaviour remain unverified.

## Decisions carried forward

| Decision | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Complete plans before pictures | The foundation and all eight screen documents precede image generation. |
| One shared portal with operational variation | Common shell/components, with domain schemas and workflows selected by validated answers. No requirement for 128 independent codebases or cosmetic redesigns. |
| Every current specialty explicitly covered | 128 named profiles, eight screen mappings each and named acceptance fixtures. |
| Future categories supported | One versioned authoring manifest composes existing capabilities and publishes form/portal projections atomically. Novel behaviour requires its own extension before activation. |
| Separate business configuration and effective permissions | A valid merchant answer cannot grant access, connect a provider or confirm a transaction. |
| Preserve previous work | Started work and accepted terms retain their versions and historical references. |
| External systems first, future ERP compatible | Portal contracts isolate providers; future native capabilities support the same business interfaces and per-class authority. |
| Context governs UX principles | Necessary information remains available; hierarchy, grouping and relevant disclosure organize it. No arbitrary feature limits or mandatory empty dashboard cards. |

## Cross-screen consistency checks

- Work distinguishes requested, confirmed, fulfilled and financially settled facts. Availability owns capacity; Money owns financial state.
- Offerings preserves stable identities and external references; imports are non-destructive and partial outcomes inspectable.
- Business reuses onboarding questions/configuration and owns operational setup. Settings owns personal/security/preferences and connection journeys; duplicate business facts are linked, not copied.
- Promotions is separate from Insights and uses only approved campaign operations. Insights explains metric definitions, coverage and comparability.
- All nine navigation destinations including Home remain reachable on phone. Help, messaging and support use real permission-scoped channels and destinations.
- Sources, units, currency, timezone and location scope remain explicit. Unknown is never inferred as zero, false, free capacity, paid or completed.
- A driver option adds rental resource requirements; a separately selected transfer capability adds transfer work. Home-service availability does not create a visit child for every venue booking.
- All dependency conditions compile to one bounded expression representation. General scripts, SQL, unrestricted paths and permission grants are prohibited.

## Resolved review findings

1. Aligned condition grammar across the question registry, category manifest and configuration plan. Unknown remains unknown under inequality; unsupported negation/numeric predicates are not implied.
2. Made readiness claims precise in planning fixtures. Answer completeness and source/grant checks are separate from operational readiness and current task-state guards.
3. Added explicit guarded workflow variants rather than relying on prose that says steps can be skipped. Quote approval is separate from financial prerequisites, and operational cancellation can retain a separately pending refund.
4. Preserved a distinction between enabled workflow definitions and actual instantiated linked work, so conditional capabilities do not duplicate charges or reservations.
5. Primary workflow changes recompute work vocabulary, offering/availability variants and report definitions. A no-booking service job cannot retain an appointment label or appointment-only metrics.
6. Closed domain references: 17 offering schemas, 16 availability models, 45 metric definitions, 61 business capabilities and 19 fulfilment methods, including explicit mappings for all configured mode aliases and the ten fulfilment question values.

## Owner decisions and future implementation gates

| Owner/dependency | Design treatment until confirmed |
| --- | --- |
| Identity roles, protected fields, authentication and last-owner policy | Reference approved grants/commands; no new live privileges granted by plan metadata. |
| Finance fees, tax, rounding, deposit, refund and settlement rules | Display defined records; gate dependent actions; no invented percentage, deadline or financial rail. |
| Merchant/document approval, publication and grace policies | Show separate requirements/review states; do not self-verify claims or prescribe a new legal requirement. |
| Booking/resource/fulfilment hold, confirmation and proof contracts | Use supported owner operations; show request-only or unavailable paths when guarantees cannot be made. |
| Provider support and credentials | Validate connector capability and authorization individually; questionnaire intent is not connection readiness. |
| Retention, sensitive data and export rules | Limit to necessary administrative/operational data; runtime privacy/security review required. |
| Additional promotion types and attribution | Keep unsupported controls disabled/unavailable; define rule/funding/source before enabling. |
| Metric events, privacy and completeness | Define registered formulas and coverage; unavailable evidence produces an honest report state. |
| Referenced master specifications absent from checkout | Reconcile proposed architecture with available approved specifications before application implementation. |

These dependencies are represented by explicit states and contracts rather than silently chosen policies. Their values can remain owner-controlled while the interaction design and bounded configuration mechanism are fully specified. Pictures illustrate fictional records with confirmed demo states; they do not resolve these product policies.

## Verification boundaries

See `verification.json` for the actual planning-artifact checks and their results. Structural schema tests, ID coverage, typed references and fixture consistency are design verification. They do not prove that an application resolver, connector, command or UI behaves correctly. Runtime phases remain planned, not accepted.
